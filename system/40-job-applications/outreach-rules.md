# Outreach wording rules

How a networking message reads, warm or cold: its principles, its structure, its examples and the follow-up. The house rules (`{house_rules}`) apply in full, and a rule here that is a house rule applied to a message names its ID. The workflow that drafts the message owns its steps: which kind of connection it is, what to ask the user, the research, where the draft goes and how the outreach is logged. Which rule wins is set once, in the house rules' opening paragraph.

## Every message

- **No em or en dashes anywhere** in a drafted message, its examples and its follow-ups. Search the draft before presenting it. `HR-no-dashes`
- **No AI-style contrast or stock phrases.** They sound hollow and templated: rewrite the sentence with a specific fact or a direct statement instead. Most of them are house rules: "It's not about X, it's about Y", "Not just X, but Y" and "What makes X special is not Y, it's Z" (`HR-no-negative-parallelism`, `HR-ai-no-unasked-contrast`); "At the end of the day...", "The reality is...", "Here's the thing..." and "In a world where..." (`HR-no-stock-phrasing`); any sentence that starts with "Here is", "Here's" or "Here are" (`HR-no-here-opener`). A message also never uses "What sets X apart is..." or "More than just...".
- **First person, present tense, active voice.**
- **It sounds like the user** (see Voice).
- **One ask only,** never several layered into one message, and never "I'd love to pick your brain", which is too vague.

## A warm message

For someone with a direct shared tie: the same university, a former employer, a mutual contact.

- Lead with the shared tie immediately. It's the reason they'll read on.
- Keep it short: four to six sentences at most, under 100 words.
- Ask one genuine, low-stakes question. Not "can you refer me", not "I'm looking for a job".
- The question is curiosity-driven and easy to answer in two or three sentences.
- No hard ask. No CV. No "I'd love to pick your brain".
- Warm but not sycophantic, and human, not transactional: the question is specific and genuinely curious, not a veiled job ask.

Structure:
1. Opening: name the shared tie directly (one sentence).
2. Brief context: who the user is and why they're reaching out (one or two sentences).
3. A genuine question about their experience (one sentence, specific to them or their company).
4. A simple close.

## A cold message

For someone who works at a target company in a role related to the user's targets, with no direct shared connection. **Anchor the message on one specific, real detail** about them: a recent article, post or talk, a product or project they led that relates to the user's work, an interesting career transition, a shared perspective on their domain. It is the most important part of a cold outreach. `HR-never-invent`

The principles come from *Reach Out* by Molly Beck:
- **Lead with them, not you.** Open with something specific about their work or perspective, not your ask, and never with "I came across your profile".
- **Make a genuine observation.** Reference something real: an article, a decision, a talk, a career move. Never fake it, and no fake compliments: the observation is real and specific.
- **Be specific about what you want.** A 20-minute call, a quick question, their honest take. Never "pick your brain."
- **Make it easy to say yes.** Keep the request small and bounded (time-limited, low effort).
- **Offer value or reciprocity.** Even a small one: share a relevant resource, offer a perspective, connect them with someone.
- **Keep it short.** Under 150 words. If it's longer, cut it.
- **One ask only.** Never layer multiple asks into one message.
- **Don't apologize for reaching out.** Be confident and direct.

Structure:
1. Specific opening: what caught the user's attention about them (one or two sentences, and it must be real).
2. Brief context: who the user is and the relevant connection (one or two sentences).
3. The ask: small, specific, time-bounded (one sentence).
4. Optional: a value offer or an easy opt-out (one sentence).

## Example messages

These are examples, labelled as examples (`HR-label-examples`). Fill [Your first name], [current title], [current company] and [University] from the user's profile (`{profile}`), and [target roles] and [target direction] from their goals (`GOALS.md`). Never hardcode a name or an employer.

**Warm, a fellow alum:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> I noticed we both studied at [University]. Small world! I'm currently exploring [target roles] in [city] and came across your work at [Company].
>
> I'm curious: do you enjoy working there? I'd love to hear your honest take on the culture and the kind of work you're doing.
>
> No pressure at all. Happy to connect either way.
>
> [Your first name]

**Warm, a shared ex-employer:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> I see we both worked at [Company]. Looks like we overlapped around [year]. I'm currently exploring [target roles] and noticed you made the move to [Current Company]. How has that transition been for you?
>
> Always good to reconnect with people from that world.
>
> [Your first name]

**Cold, anchored on their article or post:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> I read your post on [topic] last week. Your point about [specific insight] stuck with me. I'm working on something similar at [current company] and it reframed how I'm thinking about [related challenge].
>
> I'm [Your first name], [current title] at [current company], exploring my next move into [target direction]. [Company]'s approach to [specific thing] is exactly the kind of work I'm looking to move toward.
>
> Would you be open to a 20-minute call to share what the product culture is like there? Happy to return the favor and share what I've learned [from your own work].
>
> [Your first name]

**Cold, anchored on their career path:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Your move from [previous role] to [current role] at [Company] caught my eye. It looks like a path I'm actively thinking through.
>
> I'm [Your first name], currently [one-line scope of current role] at [current company]. I'm exploring [target roles], and [Company]'s work on [specific product/area] is genuinely interesting to me.
>
> Would you be open to a quick 20-minute conversation? I'd love to hear your honest take on what it's like to work there.
>
> [Your first name]

**Cold, anchored on their talk or public work:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> I watched your talk on [topic] from [conference/event]. The section on [specific point] was sharp, and I've been recommending it to my team.
>
> I'm [Your first name], [current title] at [current company], looking at my next chapter in [target direction]. [Company] is on my shortlist for specific reasons I'd be happy to share.
>
> Would you have 20 minutes to talk about what the work is actually like from the inside?
>
> [Your first name]

## The follow-up

If there is no response after five to seven days, one follow-up is appropriate. One follow-up only: if no response comes after that, move on.

**Warm follow-up:**
> Hi [Name], just bumping this up in case it got lost. No pressure either way. Happy to connect whenever works for you.

**Cold follow-up:**
> Hi [Name], following up on my message from last week. I know inboxes get full. If timing is off, no worries at all.
