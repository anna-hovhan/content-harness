# Cover letter wording rules

How a cover letter reads: its shape, how it opens, matches, argues and closes, and how it comes to sound like the user. The house rules (`{house_rules}`) apply in full, and a rule here that is a house rule applied to a cover letter names its ID. The workflow that writes the letter owns its steps (the company research it reads first, the questions it asks the user), the letter's format and where it is saved. Which rule wins is set once, in the house rules' opening paragraph.

## Shape

- **Three to four paragraphs, under 400 words:** the opening hook (two or three sentences), the match, the angle, and a close of two sentences.
- **Conversational but professional,** in the user's own voice (see Voice).
- **Every claim is backed by evidence** from the user's own records, and at least one result is quantified. `HR-never-invent`, `HR-checkable-claims`

## The opening hook

Make the reader want to keep reading: show company-specific knowledge in the first two sentences, then connect it at once to why this role is interesting to the user specifically. The hook is one specific thing about the company, its product or its strategy that is genuinely interesting, never "I admire your mission".

Strong hook patterns:
- A specific product decision or feature: "When [Company] launched [X], it was a signal that..."
- A market shift the company is positioned for: "The shift toward [trend] is creating exactly the kind of [opportunity] that [Company]'s [product] is built for..."
- Something the user genuinely noticed as a user or observer.

Weak hook patterns, never used:
- "I am writing to apply for the [role] at [Company]"
- "I have always been passionate about..."
- "Your company's innovative approach to..."
- Anything that could be sent to any company.

## The match

Connect two or three specific experiences to the company's specific needs: name the challenge or need from the JD, give a concrete example from the user's own records that addresses it, and quantify the result.

Sentence patterns to use:
- "At [Company], I [action verb] [what], resulting in [outcome]."
- "When [Company] faced [similar challenge], I [action]. The result: [metric]."

Sentence patterns to avoid:
- "I believe I would be a great fit because..."
- "I have experience in..." (too passive)
- Any adjective without evidence ("strategic", "passionate", "collaborative").

## The angle

Show strategic thinking about the company's business: it separates a candidate who read the JD from one who understands the company. Identify one strategic question or opportunity in their space, show how the user's background gives them a specific perspective on it, and connect it to what they would focus on in the role. Keep it under 250 words. It reads as the opening of a strategy conversation, not a job application.

## The close

Confident, direct and warm; not desperate, not formulaic. One sentence of genuine enthusiasm, specific rather than generic; one sentence that invites the conversation or the next step, confident rather than pleading; then a simple sign-off.

- **Avoid:** "I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience", which is stiff and passive.
- **Better:** "I'd love to talk about how this translates to what you're building.", which is direct and human.

## Words

- **No generic phrases:** "results-driven", "passionate about", "proven track record". `HR-ai-keep-specific-fact`
- **No adjective without evidence.** `HR-ai-keep-specific-fact`
- **Direct and specific, never vague and corporate,** with no filler words.
- **No em or en dashes anywhere** in the letter, from the hook to the sign-off. `HR-no-dashes`

## Voice

- Read the user's own tone of voice guide, `{tone_of_voice}`, first when it exists (who they write for, the qualities of their voice, their grammar), and follow it.
- Then read the writing samples in `{voice_samples}`: every file directly in it except `README.md` and dotfiles. Its subfolders of social media posts (`{social_samples}`, `{social_inspiration}`) are for LinkedIn posts only, never a tone reference for a letter.
- With no samples, say so once, in one line ("no voice samples yet, so this follows the Voice and Tone section of your profile: drop a few things you've written into [the samples folder, `{voice_samples}`] and the next draft will sound more like you"), then carry on from the Voice and Tone section of the user's profile (`{profile}`). Never block the letter on it, and never ask the user to describe their own voice.
- After drafting, check the letter against the samples: it sounds like the user's actual writing, direct and specific. Read it aloud; if it doesn't sound natural, rewrite it.

- [ ] It sounds like the user (the guide and the samples, as Voice says)
