# CV wording rules

How the words of a CV read: the summary, the bullets, and the person and tense they are written in. The house rules (`{house_rules}`) apply in full, and a rule here that is a house rule applied to a CV names its ID. The workflow that writes the CV owns everything else: its steps, the layout and formatting (font, bullets, spacing, the two-page length and its cut order, the export), the ATS rules and the file names. Which rule wins is set once, in the house rules' opening paragraph.

## Person and tense

- **First person, never third.** "Own the product roadmap", "Lead cross-functional teams", "Hold dual mandate"; never "Owns the product roadmap", "She leads cross-functional teams" or "Holds dual mandate". The test: if "she" or "her" could replace the subject, it is third person, so rewrite it.
- **Tense follows the role's dates.** The current role, the one whose dates end in "Present", takes the present tense for ongoing work (Own, Lead, Build, Drive) and the past tense for completed achievements (Launched, Facilitated). Past roles take the past tense throughout (Owned, Led, Built, Drove), so a bullet written while its role was current is converted. Either way it is "Own" or "Owned", never "Owns". The summary follows the same dates: only the current role reads as current.

## Bullets

- **Each bullet opens with a strong, varied action verb:** Led, Built, Designed, Launched, Drove, Reduced, Increased, Defined, Partnered. Never "Responsible for", "Helped with" or "Worked on".
- **Quantify:** every bullet that can carry a metric carries one. `HR-checkable-claims`
- **The JD's language where it is true:** replace generic wording with the JD's keywords where the experience supports them. `HR-never-invent`
- **Reframe, never invent:** a bullet reframes and emphasises what the user's master CV and assets already hold, and a new claim or metric waits for the user's confirmation. `HR-never-invent`, `HR-checkable-claims`

## The summary

- Lead with the most relevant aspect of the user's background for this role.
- Include one or two of the JD's keywords, naturally.
- End with a forward-looking statement that connects to this role.
- Take the strongest phrases from whichever summary variant fits; a tighter rewrite that serves the role beats a verbatim copy.
- **No generic phrases:** "results-driven", "passionate about", "proven track record". Use specific nouns and verbs from the JD and real achievements. `HR-ai-keep-specific-fact`

## Tone

- **Confident and specific, not adjective-heavy:** no adjective without evidence. `HR-ai-keep-specific-fact`
- **No em or en dashes anywhere**, headings, bullets, date ranges and the summary included: rewrite the sentence. `HR-no-dashes`

## Self-check

- [ ] Every bullet and sentence is in the first person (own, lead, build; never owns, leads, builds, never "she")
- [ ] Tense follows the role's dates: only the current role reads as current, in the summary too
- [ ] The summary leads with the most relevant aspect of the user's background for this role
- [ ] Every bullet opens with a strong action verb, and every bullet that can carry a metric has one
- [ ] The JD's keywords appear where the experience supports them
- [ ] No generic phrase, no adjective without evidence; the tone is confident and specific
- [ ] No em or en dashes anywhere (`HR-no-dashes`)
- [ ] No invented or exaggerated experience (`HR-never-invent`)
